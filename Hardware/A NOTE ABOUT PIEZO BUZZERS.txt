A NOTE ABOUT PIEZO BUZZERS

The specified part, Adafruit P/N 1536, is different from the original part and draws much too much current for the 74HCT125 driver.
Adafruit is aware of the problem but, as of this date, has not resolved the issue.  In the meantime, two alternative parts were ordered
from Digikey and evaluated:

1.  Digikey P/N 2223-CPI-137-83T-ND:  These buzzers are rated to draw 10 ma max from a 5 volt supply.  Two were ordered
	and the measured current was between 3.5 and 3.8 ma.  These buzzers will typically work in the circuit but coule draw
	a little more current than the 74HCT125 is specified to supply.

2.  Digikey P/N 28-PI-TH12-0541-RK-ND:  These buzzers are rated to draw 5 ma max from a 5 volt supply.  Two were ordered
	and the measured current was about 1.5 ma.  These buzzers will work in the circuit but are slightly lower in volume than
	the other buzzer, above.

Another alternative is a high current buzzer module that has a transistor driver on the module; e.g. 
https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYDJMP16?ref=ppx_yo2ov_dt_b_fed_asin_title&th=1.  These buzzer modules are very loud but require
+5 volts, GND and a control signal.  They won't mount directly on the PCB.  Rather:

(a) solder single male pin headers to the + and - of the buzzer on the PCB.
(b) solder a single pin header to the +5 on the XPND header field.
(c) wire the buzzer - to the module GND pin
(d) wire the buzzer + to the I/O control pin of the module,
(e) wire the +5 on the XPND field to the module Vcc pin.

NOTE: this module is active low, so the software must invert the polarity for the buzzer to work correctly.
NOTE: this module must be mounted externally to the PCB and female-female jumper wires are needed to connect it to the PCB.
